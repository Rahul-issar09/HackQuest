# 2. TCS Rules and Content Boundaries

This document separates contest facts supported by the participant-uploaded **TCS HackQuest Season 11 2026 Terms and Conditions** from the product team's preparation recommendations.

## 2.1 Rules stated in the uploaded TCS document

### Eligibility and participation
- The contest is open to students enrolled in a recognised Indian college/university/institute who will graduate in academic year 2027, in the degree categories listed in the rules.
- Participation is individual; team/group participation is not allowed.
- Registration requires a valid TCS CT/DT reference ID generated through the IT section of TCS NextStep.
- The participant must check the official contest site for updates.

### Contest structure
- Round 1: online CTF.
- Round 2: online and remotely proctored; camera must remain on for the entire Round 2.
- Round 3: in-person at TCS premises; selected participants explain their approach and answer jury questions.

### Round 1 categories described by TCS
**Beginner**
- Fundamental cybersecurity concepts.
- Fundamental tools and techniques.
- Examples include password cracking, simple encryption and basic network vulnerabilities.

**Intermediate**
- Problem-solving and creative thinking.
- Advanced cybersecurity tools and methodologies, including usage and nuances of AI/ML.
- Examples include web application security, SQL injection, SOC detection and response scenarios, and network analysis.
- Deeper understanding of common vulnerabilities and exploitation techniques.

**Expert**
- Resourcefulness and deep cybersecurity understanding.
- Examples include reverse engineering, advanced cryptography and sophisticated penetration testing.

### Round 1 mechanics and evaluation
- Round 1 lasts six hours.
- Participants solve challenge statements and submit captured flags through the online portal.
- After solving a challenge, participants must capture a screenshot of the flag with the timestamp visible.
- A detailed step-by-step report using the template provided on the contest site must be uploaded before the site's deadline.
- Report upload is mandatory; failure to upload results in disqualification from further evaluation.
- Reports must be original and comply with the maximum file size on the site.
- Tools that send huge amounts of traffic or tamper with the contest site/applications are prohibited.
- Evaluation criteria listed: flags captured, quality of report and approach followed to capture flags.

### Round 2 examples
The rules list system exploitation, vulnerabilities in AI/ML applications, web application penetration testing, mobile security, digital forensics, threat hunting, incident response, source-code analysis, SOC scenarios and identity/access scenarios.

### Round 3 domains that may provide an advantage
The rules list application security, network security, Java/J2EE, PHP, Python, Go, information security and risk management, ethical hacking, AI red teaming, data analytics/data science, AI/ML security, digital forensics, threat hunting, malware analysis/reverse engineering and security certifications.

### Conduct restrictions
The rules prohibit seeking guidance, pep-up, technology or language-specific sessions from another person during the contest. The application is for preparation before the contest and must not be used to provide prohibited assistance during the contest.

## 2.2 Content boundaries

The app must label content using these classifications:

- **Officially mentioned for Round 1:** topic is explicitly present in the uploaded Round 1 category descriptions.
- **Supplementary practice:** useful practice chosen to build capability for a broad category, but not named by TCS.
- **Mentioned for later rounds:** appears in Round 2 or Round 3 rules, not specifically in Round 1 category descriptions.
- **Unverified exam relevance:** no claim that the topic will appear.

Examples:
- SQL injection: Officially mentioned for Round 1 Intermediate.
- Network analysis: Officially mentioned for Round 1 Intermediate.
- SOC detection/response: Officially mentioned for Round 1 Intermediate.
- Reverse engineering and advanced cryptography: Officially mentioned for Round 1 Expert.
- Password cracking and simple encryption: Officially mentioned for Round 1 Beginner.
- Digital forensics and mobile security: mentioned in Round 2; do not label as confirmed Round 1 topics.
- XSS, SSRF, IDOR, LFI, file upload, Wireshark, PCAP, Ghidra, ExifTool and steganography: supplementary practice unless independently confirmed by a current official TCS source.

## 2.3 No invented official facts

Do not hard-code or claim:
- Exact number of Round 1 challenges.
- Exact points per challenge.
- Exact category distribution.
- Exact pass mark or qualification threshold.
- Exact number of participants.
- Guaranteed topics or question types.
- Guaranteed prizes, interviews or job offers.

These details may change and must be verified on the official TCS site if needed.
